Frequently Asked Questions

What is art therapy?

Art therapy considers art-making a central pathway of communication and healing alongside the psychotherapeutic relationship. Art can free the imagination and enhance transformation in the lives of clients in a way distinct to art psychotherapy through evidence-based interventions that impact change and transformation supported by culture-centered care. Art augments the therapeutic alliance, activates the brain in a unique way to enhance the effectiveness of interventions, and provides a culturally sensitive foundation for interaction.

Who might benefit from art therapy?

Art therapy is easily tailored to face the wide-ranging, culturally diverse needs of many people. People can benefit across different ages and stages of life. Clients can participate in art therapy as both individuals, in small group settings, with family, in an open studio experience, and in community-based art opportunities.

What can I expect in an art therapy session?

Your time in therapy is structured based on your unique needs. Based on your concerns, goals, and comfort level, creative work and talk therapy can be included in different ways. Creative work is more about the process and each art therapist is trained to meet diverse abilities with different art materials, forms of movement, and types of activity. During your initial phone call, we can share more about what we offer and will ask about your interests to help you move toward meeting your needs.

Do I have to commit to a certain number of sessions?

No–there is no obligation to commit. However, we encourage participants to try to attend two art therapy sessions before making a decision about their path forward.

What are the benefits of group art therapy?

Group art therapy emphasizes the process of creating art alongside the presence of others to create opportunities for connection, shared creativity, and coregulation. The goal is to use the art to gain insight into your own personal lived experiences and, then, share at whatever level feels comfortable to you. Words aren’t always the emphasis and, instead, the art supports the group members and therapist in expressing and exploring what is coming up in their creative process. The emphasis is on having fun and trying your best!

How is an Open Studio different from group art therapy?

The open studio approach to art therapy emphasizes materials exploration, less structured art-making, and the process of creating art alongside the presence of others to create opportunities for connection, shared creativity, and coregulation. The goal is to use the art in your own individual work to gain insight into your own personal lived experiences and, then, gently reflect on the experience of making. Words aren’t always the emphasis and, instead, the art supports the group members and therapist in expressing and exploring what is coming up in their creative process. The emphasis is on having fun and trying your best!

How does someone become an art therapist?

Registered Art Therapists are no longer students in school. They are highly trained post-graduate mental health professionals. They have already completed three years of a rigorous masters degree program, in which they had to complete 1,000 hours of clinical experience before graduation. During this time, they receive training on counseling techniques, the Expressive Therapies Continuum and Media Dimension Variables, and art-based assessments. After graduation, they receive their ATR-P national credential and a provisional state license. This is when the period of clinical supervision begins. During the clinical supervision period, they complete an additional 1,000 hours of direct client contact hours before receiving their ATR. Throughout these hours, they are supervised either on or off site by a Board Certified Art Therapist Supervisor who has a minimum of five-years post license experience and has also taken another rigorous course in order to be certified to supervise. The ATR you see may also sit for the board exam and become an ATR-BC which is the highest national certification awarded by the Art Therapy Credentials Board. An ATR may discuss your case confidentially with their supervisor. When you work with an ATR who is practicing under supervision, you receive more than the knowledge and experience of one clinician. You also benefit from the consultation and expertise of an experienced art therapy supervisor. This collaborative approach supports thoughtful, ethical, and high-quality care, often while allowing services to be offered at a more accessible rate.

Can a person do both art therapy and talk therapy?

Absolutely! Curiosity is part of the process of discovering the best way to meet your mental health needs and different types of therapists and professionals will take into integrate different forms of support into your sessions. Try a variety of forms of therapy to see what works best for you.
